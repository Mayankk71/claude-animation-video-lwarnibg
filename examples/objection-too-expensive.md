# Script — Prospect says "it's too expensive"

**Runtime:** ~13s
**Characters:** Sales Rep (Marcus — established/familiar rapport with this prospect, several calls in), Prospect (Dana — decision-holder, deadpan, checked-out register)
**Setting:** Google Meet call, both on video, home-office backgrounds (Marcus: neutral, Dana: visible bookshelf + candle/mug clutter)

## Beat map

| Beat | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open + Hook/Setup (fused) | 0:00–0:04 | Already mid-call. Marcus asks for a decision; Dana states the objection flatly, immediately — no build-up. |
| Minimal conflict | 0:04–0:06 | Marcus asks exactly one flat follow-up question. No pushback, no value pitch, no argument. |
| Build-up pause | 0:06–0:08 | The script's longest silence. Camera pushes slowly in on Dana's static face. |
| Punchline | 0:08–0:11 | Dana delivers one unbroken sentence — a hyper-specific, mundane detail that undercuts her own objection. |
| Post-punchline hold / CTA | 0:11–0:13 | Cut to Marcus's tile, flat, no reply. Hard cut to outro card. |

## Dialogue

**[0:00] Marcus:** "So — any thoughts on the proposal?"
**[0:01] Dana:** "Yeah. It's too expensive."
**[0:04] Marcus:** "Too expensive compared to what?"
**[0:06] *(silence — 2s, longest pause in the script)***
**[0:08] Dana:** "The eleven-dollar oat milk latte I bought waiting for this call to start."
**[0:11] *(silence — 2s trailing hold, no reply from Marcus)***

## On-screen text / CTA

Hard cut to static black card: **"LOW BATTERY PITCHES"** / small credit line / yellow ribbon graphic: **"NEW OBJECTION DAILY"**. No resolution shown — the call does not visibly continue.

## Why this follows the Style Bible

Fuses hook+setup into one exchange (invariant, 36/46), uses exactly one flat follow-up as the only "conflict" with zero escalation (frequent, 34/46), places the single longest pause immediately before the punchline (invariant, 43/46), and lands the punchline as one unbroken, hyper-specific-detail sentence rather than a vague exaggeration (optional pattern, 18/46 + 29/46) — irony/misdirection mechanism: a sincere-sounding budget objection undercut by a petty, specific personal expense. No reaction shot after the punchline (optional, 17/46), ends on a hard cut to a branded card with zero narrative resolution (invariant, 46/46).

# Veo3 Prompt Set — Prospect says "it's too expensive"

**Style keywords (from the Style Bible):** felted-wool/clay stop-motion-*look* CG shader (not real stop-motion — no jitter, no fingerprints, fully smooth motion); heavy-lidded "low battery" eyes, no blinking; flat downturned mouth held through emotionally loaded lines; elongated nose + small round glasses on at least one character; animation concentrated on mouth/eyebrows/eyelids only, body completely locked/static; discrete held viseme mouth poses, not fluid lip-sync; locked-off static camera except one permitted slow straight push-in landing on the punchline; warm soft single-source practical light (laptop/monitor glow) with shallow DOF; fully static background; muted desaturated color palette; no music, no SFX; deadpan low-pitch-variation delivery throughout.

## Shot 1 — Cold open / Hook+Setup (0:00–0:04)

```
Stop-motion-look 3D CG animation, felted-wool clay-texture shader on two
puppet-style characters, matte and fibrous, no glossy CG highlights, no
set jitter, no fingerprints — fully smooth CG motion underneath a
handmade-look material. Static locked-off wide shot framed as a Google
Meet split screen: two video tiles side by side. Left tile: MARCUS, a
man with an elongated wedge-shaped nose and small round glasses, heavy-
lidded half-closed eyes, flat downturned mouth at rest, sitting at a
desk lit by a warm laptop-screen glow against a plain neutral home-
office background. Right tile: DANA, a woman with the same heavy-lidded
"low battery" resting expression, seated in front of a softly blurred
bookshelf with a candle and a mug visible, shallow depth of field.
Neither character blinks. Bodies stay completely locked and static;
only mouths, eyebrows, and eyelids move, snapping between a small set
of held viseme poses as they speak. Marcus asks flatly, "So — any
thoughts on the proposal?" Dana replies immediately, same flat register,
no pause: "Yeah. It's too expensive." No music, no sound effects, dialogue
only. Camera does not move.
```
Notes: Establishing two-shot per the shot/reverse-shot default. Both characters share the same deadpan resting face and identical low-pitch delivery — no vocal or expressive differentiation between them.

## Shot 2 — Minimal conflict (0:04–0:06)

```
Same static two-tile Meet framing, same felted-wool CG-clean shader, no

"Too expensive compared to what?" No escalation in tone, no raised

```
Notes: This is the script's only "conflict" beat — one flat follow-up question, no pushback, no value-defense speech.

## Shot 3 — Build-up pause (0:06–0:08)

```

```
Notes: This silence must read as measurably longer than any other pause in the piece — it is the load-bearing comedic-timing beat per the Style Bible.

## Shot 4 — Punchline (0:08–0:11)

```
Continuous with Shot 3's tight close-up on Dana (push-in now settled
tight on her face), same static clay/felt shader, no blinking, flat
resting mouth. She delivers one unbroken sentence with no internal
pause, completely deadpan, no vocal escalation: "The eleven-dollar oat
milk latte I bought waiting for this call to start." Only her mouth and
eyebrows animate through held viseme poses; the rest of her body and the
background remain locked and static. No music, no sound effects.
```
Notes: Punchline isolates Dana in tight close-up per the invariant punchline-framing rule; delivered as one unbroken escalating-detail sentence (the optional alternative to a 1-3 word capitulation line), anchored on a hyper-specific quantified detail ($11, oat milk latte) rather than a vague exaggeration.

## Continuity notes

Keep Marcus's and Dana's character models (nose shape, glasses on Marcus, felted-wool clay shader, eye/mouth resting design), their home-office set dressing (Marcus: neutral desk + laptop glow; Dana: bookshelf + candle + mug), and the warm/muted desaturated color grade identical across all shots — the only visual variation across the whole piece should come from reframing (wide two-shot → isolated close-ups), never a location or character-design change, and never a camera movement beyond the single push-in in Shots 3–4.
